How I work

I take last year's per-seat price and move it up one step. No promotions, and volume pricing is traded for a commitment.

I open about two months before renewal with the customer's own usage, so a change in tier reads as a fit to how they work.

Change the deal before the price

Five deals

Customers are described by industry, and figures are rounded. Choose a deal to read it.

  • The order roughly doubled after an early reseller briefing

    Situation
    A legacy licence model, renewed through a reseller and a distributor. Every contact I had on the account had left or changed role.
    What I did
    Briefed the reseller six weeks before a licensing change, found new contacts through them, wrote to the security architect directly and set up a single sign-on test that ran to the renewal date.
    Result
    The customer added single sign-on and roughly doubled the order. My largest deal of the year.

    The customer was already moving licence models. I take credit for the timing and the position, not the demand.

  • A rejected increase, reshaped instead of discounted

    Situation
    PDF and e-signature had just been split into separate products, and the CFO rejected the first renewal offer.
    What I did
    Sized e-signature from their own usage, about 2,500 envelopes a year and 30 senders, and replaced the offer with a pooled plan. Concessions came in steps, each tied to one objection.
    Result
    Closed in about 39 days at roughly 40% above last year, and the customer took e-signature Enterprise for the first time.

  • Their usage made the case

    Situation
    A discontinued bundle with free e-signature, and more active users than licences.
    What I did
    Walked through their usage on an 11-minute call, confirmed it in writing the same day, and kept the PDF price where it was so the decision was about seats.
    Result
    A three-year term with more PDF seats and e-signature added, 18 days after my first email. No price objection on record.

  • Two competitive replacements

    Situation
    On a routine call I learned a parent group planned to leave Adobe, with the first contract due in weeks. Separately, a customer had let an e-signature trial lapse while evaluating DocuSign.
    What I did
    Scoped the group, wrote a dated plan for quote, reseller, PO and licences, and quoted the first company on its own so the date would hold. Reissued the lapsed trial within an hour and agreed a decision date.
    Result
    95 seats won from Adobe, PO on the planned day. The e-signature deal closed seven days after the opportunity opened, as a three-year Enterprise contract with a CRM integration.

  • A defence that became a full rollout

    Situation
    The customer wanted one PDF tool for all staff and was looking at a cheaper, lighter product, mid-contract.
    What I did
    Said plainly that we had no lightweight version, got approval for additional seats co-termed with the existing agreement in return for a full rollout, and left the existing contract price alone.
    Result
    All staff moved onto our product on the planned date, and the next renewal covers the larger seat count.

    I gave ground on the new seats' price to win the standardisation, so I do not count this one as holding price.

PDF and e-signature software is easy to swap, and competitors often sell on price. If I discount, customers learn to wait for the discount.

So I would rather give every renewal a few reasons to stay: the right tier, volume, term, an integration, or proof of how they already use it. Then price is one point of comparison among several.

I check this against my own data every month, and I keep the results that go against me.

What I am still fixing

  • Smaller renewals, US$2K to US$10K, retain less than my larger ones. I now add one direct contact 90 days out.
  • Renewals led by a partner with no direct customer contact lose more value. I contact the customer myself even when a reseller leads.
  • Whether this year's increases hold at next year's renewal. I will know in 2027.
